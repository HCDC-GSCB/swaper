Emergency Response to Epidemic

The Department of Surveillance, Warning, Preparedness, and Emergency Response is tasked with protecting public health through a structured and systematic approach. The department’s mandate is executed through three primary missions:

1. To Ensure the Quality of the Public Health Monitoring System

This mission focuses on the development and oversight of processes that guarantee the accuracy, completeness, and timeliness of data from all public health surveillance systems. It includes conducting systematic evaluations and rapid surveys to inform and continuously improve monitoring strategies.

2. To Monitor, Detect, and Alert on Unusual Health Events

This mission involves the systematic collection and management of epidemiological data to monitor health trends, forecast outcomes, and assess risks. The objective is to promptly detect unusual events, issue evidence-based alerts, and propose scientifically grounded solutions to mitigate public health threats.

3. To Prepare for and Respond to Public Health Emergencies

This mission centers on maintaining a high state of operational readiness. It includes managing the Public Health Emergency Operations Center (PHEOC) and the Provincial Rapid Response Teams (RRT), and implementing comprehensive preparedness activities such as planning, training, drills, and resource allocation for effective emergency response.

To accomplish these missions, the department is organized into two primary divisions with distinct functions:

Division of Surveillance and Warning

This division is responsible for all data collection, analysis, and risk assessment functions. Its key responsibilities include:

  • Assessing the performance and quality of existing surveillance systems.

  • Developing and implementing an integrated, digital disease surveillance system, including an early warning mechanism for schools and communities, with a target for completion by 2025.

  • Conducting formal risk assessments for endemic, zoonotic, and emerging/re-emerging infectious diseases.

  • Establishing official epidemic warning thresholds for all administrative levels (city and ward).

  • Developing and applying mathematical models for the purpose of forecasting disease outbreaks.

  • Defining and utilizing key data indicators for the evaluation of outbreak severity and response effectiveness.

Division of Preparedness and Emergency Response

This division is responsible for all operational planning, readiness, and response coordination. Its key responsibilities include:

  • Advising on and developing the legal and operational frameworks for the Public Health Emergency Operations Center (PHEOC) and the Rapid Response Teams (RRT).

  • Developing and standardizing operational guidelines and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for the PHEOC and RRTs.

  • Creating and managing the activation system for the Rapid Response Teams.

  • Overseeing the strategic planning, procurement, and maintenance of essential infrastructure, equipment, and supplies for emergency response.

  • Providing technical guidance on the development of the city’s long-term Pandemic Response Plan for 2030 and beyond.

We maintain strong working relationships with leading academic research institutions, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and other specialized departments within the Ho Chi Minh City Center for Disease Control (HCDC).

These partnerships are vital for conducting cutting-edge research, validating new methodologies, and strengthening the city’s overall public health framework. Notable partners include the Oxford University Clinical Research Unit (OUCRU) and FHI 360.

A selection of key research projects undertaken through these collaborations includes:

  • Evaluation of the Reproduction Number (Rt) in Measles Outbreaks in HCMC.

  • Methods for Determining Epidemic Thresholds in Endemic Disease Surveillance: A Scoping Review and Benchmark.

  • Dengue Advanced Readiness Tools (DART).

  • Assessment of the Infectious Disease and Public Health Event Surveillance System in HCMC.

  • A Review of Methodological Approaches to Infectious Disease Risk Assessment.

  • Event-Based Surveillance Using Social Media, Google Search Data, and Online News Media.
